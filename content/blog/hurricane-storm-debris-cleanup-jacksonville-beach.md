Homes at the Beaches do not have basements to flood. They have yards, garages, lanais and fences that sit in the path of whatever comes off the Atlantic. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30, and a tropical storm or a strong nor'easter can leave a mess without ever making the national news.

This guide has two halves: what to clear before a storm, and what to do with the debris after one.

## Before the storm: clear what can fly

Anything loose in a yard can become a projectile in high wind. The best time to deal with it is on a calm week, not the day before landfall. Walk the property and look for:

- Broken patio furniture, umbrellas and old grills
- A shed that is already leaning or missing panels
- Loose or rotted fence sections
- A trampoline or playset nobody uses
- Stacked lumber, pavers and leftover project material
- Dead palm fronds and brush piles
- Old appliances, tires and scrap along the side of the house

If it is junk, get it gone. If it is worth keeping, bring it inside or tie it down.

Two more things help:

- **Clear the garage enough to use it.** In a storm the garage is where the car, the patio set and the grill need to go. If it is full, see [garage cleanouts](/services/garage-cleanouts).
- **Do not leave a pile at the curb.** A debris pile sitting out when a storm arrives becomes part of the storm. If a pickup is not certain to happen in time, keep it secured or have it hauled.

A failing shed or fence is easier to take down on purpose than to collect from the neighbor's yard. See [shed and fence removal](/services/shed-and-fence-removal) and our guide to [shed removal in Jacksonville Beach](/blog/shed-removal-jacksonville-beach).

One honest note: the days right before a forecast storm are the busiest and least predictable for any hauler. Do not count on a last-minute slot. If something in your yard worries you, deal with it early in the season.

## After the storm: safety first

Before you move anything:

- **Stay away from downed lines.** Treat every downed line as live and report it to your utility.
- **Leave hanging limbs alone.** A limb that is hung up in a tree, resting on a roof or touching a wire is a job for a licensed tree service, not for a homeowner and not for a junk hauler.
- **Take photos first.** If you are filing an insurance claim, photograph the damage before anything is moved, and ask your insurer what they need you to keep.
- **Watch your footing.** Storm piles hide nails, glass and sharp metal.

## City pickup or a hauler?

After a significant storm, cities and counties often run their own debris collection. How it works, what they accept, how it has to be sorted and when the trucks come all vary by storm and by municipality, so we are not going to state it here. Check with the City of Jacksonville Beach, your own city if you live in Neptune Beach or Atlantic Beach, or St. Johns County if you are in Ponte Vedra or Nocatee. If you live in an HOA, ask the association too, since some arrange their own cleanup or have rules about curbside piles.

Public pickup can be the right choice if you can wait and the pile fits their rules. A hauler makes sense when:

- You want the debris gone on your schedule and not on a waiting list
- The pile is in a backyard and cannot easily get to the curb
- The load is mixed, with fencing, furniture and brush together
- A rental or a home for sale needs to be presentable again
- Your HOA wants the driveway and curb cleared
