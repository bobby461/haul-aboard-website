## The decision we make on every single load

Every item that goes on the truck lands in one of three buckets: donate, recycle, or licensed disposal. In that order.

Donate first, because a working item somebody else could use shouldn't get crushed. Recycle second, because a lot of what's left is raw material pretending to be trash. Licensed disposal last, for the stuff that's genuinely done.

That sort happens whether you ask for it or not. Eco-friendly disposal isn't a paid upgrade or an add-on line item — it's just how the job runs.

## What gets donated, and what disqualifies an item

Usable furniture, working small appliances, dishes, tools, sporting goods, unopened household items. If it works and somebody would be glad to have it, it goes to a local shelter or donation center instead of a landfill.

But donation centers have standards, and they should. Here's what takes an item off the table:

- **Stains, odors, or rips.** A couch with a torn cushion isn't a gift. It's a chore you handed somebody else.
- **Missing parts.** A dresser without drawers. A table without legs. A grill without grates.
- **Safety recalls.** Recalled cribs, car seats, and similar items can't be passed along. Full stop.
- **Mattresses in poor condition.** Most donation centers won't take a used mattress at all, and the ones that do want it clean and structurally sound.

The single most useful thing you can do is tell us what still works. If that dryer ran fine last week and you're only replacing it because you upgraded, say so. We'd rather find it a second home than a scrap pile. We just can't tell by looking.

## What gets recycled, and why metal matters

Scrap metal, copper, electronics, and clean cardboard all have real recycling value and real recycling paths. Metal is the big one. It can be melted down and used again more or less indefinitely, which isn't true of most materials.

Copper especially. Old wiring, plumbing, motor windings, the guts of a water heater. Worth recovering, and recovering it means less has to come out of the ground in the first place.

Electronics get pulled for the opposite reason: they contain things you really don't want in the general waste stream, plus metals worth recapturing. Old TVs, computers, monitors, cables — those go to a licensed recycling facility rather than getting tossed in with everything else.

If you've got a pile of it sitting around, that's exactly what our [scrap metal removal](/services/scrap-metal-removal) crew is for.

## Appliances and refrigerant recovery

Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, and dehumidifiers all contain refrigerant. That refrigerant has to be recovered at a licensed facility before the unit gets scrapped. It's not optional — it's the law — and it also happens to be the right thing to do.

So when we take a fridge on an [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal) job, it goes to a facility equipped to pull the refrigerant properly. After that, the metal shell, the compressor, and the copper lines are all recyclable.

If somebody offers to take your fridge and gets vague about where it's headed, that's a flag. Ask.

## Mattresses

Mattresses are bulky, awkward, and mostly recyclable — a frustrating combination, because most of them never get recycled.

A mattress is springs, foam, fabric, and a wood frame. Springs are steel. The frame is wood. The foam has uses. Where a facility is available to take them apart, that's where ours go. Where it isn't, they go to licensed disposal, and we'll say so straight instead of pretending otherwise.

Same story for box springs and bed frames on any [furniture and mattress removal](/services/furniture-and-mattress-removal) job.

## Construction debris and yard waste

Renovation debris sorts better than almost anything else, because it shows up already separated by material. Clean wood, metal studs and ductwork, clean cardboard from packaging — all of that has somewhere to go. Mixed and contaminated material doesn't. That's the whole difference between a load that mostly gets recycled and one that mostly doesn't, and it usually gets decided on the job site before we ever pull up. Our [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal) crews pull out what's recoverable.

Yard waste is organic. Branches, palm fronds, brush, leaves, trimmings. It composts or mulches. It doesn't belong in a landfill, and on our [yard waste removal](/services/yard-waste-removal) jobs it doesn't go to one when there's a better option.

## The part nobody likes to admit

Some things can't be donated. Some things can't be recycled. Broken pressboard furniture, contaminated material, torn-up carpet, plenty of ordinary household junk. It goes to a licensed disposal facility, because that's honestly where it belongs.

We're not going to tell you nothing we haul ever reaches a landfill. That claim isn't true when anybody makes it. What's true: we route as much as we can to donation and recycling first, and what's left goes to a licensed facility — properly, not into a ditch behind somebody's shop.

Honest beats impressive.

## What we won't take at all

Short list, short for good reasons:

- **Wet paint.** Dry it out first. Lid off, ventilated spot, kitty litter or a hardening agent speeds it up. Once it's solid, we can take it.
- **Gasoline and other flammable liquids.**
- **Asbestos.** That's abatement work, not junk removal.
- **Medical waste.**
- **Live ammunition.**

## Why a crew that sorts beats a dumpster you fill and forget

A dumpster is one container going to one place. Everything you throw in gets the same destination — a working armchair, a copper water heater, a bag of drywall dust, all identical as far as the container's concerned. There's no sorting step, because there's nobody there to sort.

When a crew loads it, somebody's laying eyes on every item. That's the difference. It's also easier on your back and your weekend, which matters on the bigger jobs like [estate and garage cleanouts](/services/estate-cleanouts).

### Three things that make the sort easier

- Pile metal separately if it's already separated. Saves us a step.
- Keep donatables clean and dry. Rain on a good couch overnight is what turns it into trash.
- Tell us what still works. Seriously. Nobody can tell a working dishwasher from a dead one by looking at it.
