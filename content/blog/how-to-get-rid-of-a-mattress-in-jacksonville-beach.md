## Option 1: Donate it (only if it's genuinely clean)

If your mattress is in good shape, no stains, no tears, no odors, and definitely no bed bugs, donation is the best outcome. A few things to know first:

Most donation centers around the Beaches are picky about mattresses, and many won't take them at all. Charities have been burned too many times by mattresses that looked fine in a doorway and weren't. Call before you load anything, ask specifically whether they're accepting mattresses that week, and be honest about condition.

If it has any staining or damage, skip this option. Dropping an unusable mattress at a donation center doesn't help anyone; it just makes your problem their problem, and they end up paying to dispose of it.

## Option 2: Check your trash service's bulk pickup

Some haulers and municipalities around Duval and St. Johns County offer bulk item pickup, sometimes included, sometimes for an extra fee, and usually with rules: the mattress may need to be sealed in a plastic mattress bag, you may need to schedule it in advance, and there's often a limit of one or two bulk items per pickup.

This can be the cheapest route if your hauler offers it and you don't mind waiting for the scheduled date and wrestling the mattress to the curb yourself. Call your trash company and ask three questions: do you take mattresses, does it cost extra, and does it need to be bagged.

## Option 3: Haul it to the transfer station yourself

Local transfer stations and landfill facilities accept mattresses from residents for a fee. It works if you have a truck, a second set of hands, straps, and a free morning. Weigh that against reality: a queen mattress is awkward, a king is worse, and a box spring on the highway is a tarp-and-straps situation you want to get right. Check the facility's site for current hours, locations, and fees before you go, because showing up wrong wastes the trip.

## Option 4: Sell or give it away (be careful here)

Marketplace and Craigslist are full of free mattress listings, and most of them sit there for weeks. Used mattresses are a hard giveaway because nobody can verify what they're getting. If you try this route, take bright, honest photos, list it as porch pickup, and set a personal deadline. If it hasn't moved in a week, it's not moving.
